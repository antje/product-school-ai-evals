## 4.0 Release Criteria

The following thresholds must be met by Model Candidate v1.x before approval for production deploy. Eval Specs from Module 3 define the measurement methodology.

| Severity | Metric | Threshold | Dataset | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Hard (Blocker) | Unsupported-claim rate: the share of factual claims with no supporting span in the retrieved source (the fabricated-specifics P0) | = 0%: zero human-confirmed unsupported claims in 300 | 300-claim held-out audit, with the pricing source snapshotted and timestamped for the audit | M3 Eval Spec (`03-eval-suites/lab-2-eval-spec.md`). The judge splits each answer into single factual claims and flags unsupported ones; a human reviewer confirms the split and every flag, and only confirmed claims count. On the calibration set the judge (κ 0.824) missed none of the four ungrounded answers. Every price is also checked in code against the snapshot. A failed audit is re-run on a fresh 300, never the same set. Not the 20-row beta log: zero failures in 20 only bounds the rate under 15%, zero in 300 bounds it under 1% at 95% confidence. The 300-claim audit does not exist yet, which is the schedule risk for launch |
| 🟡 Soft (Review) | Latency, P95, from request received to last token returned, server-side | ≤ 2.0s, the target from Eng's performance log. Override band: 2.0s to 10s ships only under the staged rollout in 4.2, with PM and Eng Lead sign-off in the release ticket, and the override expires at wave 2. Above 10s: blocked, no override, because ten seconds is the response-time limit past which users lose their attention on the task (Nielsen's response-time limits) | Load test of at least 1,000 requests from the frozen production query mix at full 50-account concurrency; 1,000 requests leave 50 above the P95, enough to read the tail | Server-side timing |
| 🔵 Advisory (Monitor) | Brand voice in drafted emails | Mean ≥ 4.0 / 5 over 50 drafts; a mean below 4.0 opens a review ticket within a week. Slang-list hits warn at the PR | 50 drafted outbound emails per release; 50 puts the mean within about ±0.3 at 95%, assuming ratings spread about one point | LLM judge on a tone rubric, trusted only after it reaches κ ≥ 0.6 against human ratings, plus the PR keyword check. Assumes a human sends every drafted email; if emails ever go out automatically, this becomes a Soft gate |

## 4.1 CI Gate Policy

These thresholds run in a GitHub Actions gate on every pull request, replaying deterministic fixtures from the regression golden set (≥ 30 cases). PM owns the policy; Engineering owns the YAML.

> Per dimension, never one blended score. Faithfulness (floor 95, max regression 3), task completion (89, 3), tool selection (87, 5) and safety (98, 1) block the merge; latency (70, 3) and cost (70, 5) warn only. A full flip of one case moves a score 3.3 points on the 30-case set, so read as pass counts the floors are 29 of 30 for faithfulness, 27 of 30 for task completion and tool selection, and 30 of 30 for safety, meaning zero safety flips; this assumes scores are pass rates, while the harness reports averaged per-case scores. Floors are fixed numbers set against today's `main`; they ratchet up when `main` improves and never come down without a PM decision. The baseline is `main` at the PR's merge-base. CI allows one faithfulness case short of perfect because it exists to catch regressions; the Hard release gate, zero in 300, certifies the rate. Runs must repeat: temperature 0, pinned agent and judge versions, each case run three times with a majority vote, no manual re-runs and no admin bypass on a blocking dimension. Inputs and retrieved context are frozen fixtures, so a red check reflects the change rather than a different input. Two further checks run on every PR: the price check against the pricing snapshot, which blocks, and the slang-list check, which warns. Latency and cost appear on the demo's 0 to 100 scale; Engineering maps them to milliseconds at P95 and dollars per task before wiring. Applied to PR #218, this policy blocks the merge on a 9-point faithfulness regression that a blended score would have passed, and warns on the latency and cost regressions that the template thresholds hid (`04-eval-gates/lab-ci-gate-policy.md`).

## 4.2 Mitigation Plan · Soft Gate

**Selected Lever:** Staged Rollout

> If our Soft Gate fails (answers take 4.2 seconds against our 2-second target), we recommend **Staged Rollout** because it limits how many clients feel the wait while we fix it: 10 of the 50 accounts get access first, so if the wait puts people off, it affects a fifth of the accounts rather than all fifty. Four seconds still beats the hours these clients spend digging today, and anything over ten seconds does not ship at all. A beta label would not limit who is affected, and a delay would give up the quarter over speed rather than accuracy. Accuracy still has to pass in full before the first account gets access. For Engineering: a per-account feature flag; wave 1 is the 10 accounts that asked the most questions in the beta, so their tail shows up fastest; wave 1 measures real P95 and the share of questions abandoned or retried within a minute, which is the "puts people off" signal; wave 2 opens after seven days at or under 2.0s P95 with no rise in that share; the flag comes off for the wave if P95 passes 10s or the share doubles, pulled by the Eng Lead on call. Wave 1 runs at a fifth of full load, so it shows whether real questions are slower than the test mix, not what full load does; that number comes from the 50-account load test. The first latency fix to pair with the rollout is streaming the first token, which cuts the felt wait before the total time falls.
